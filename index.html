Jal Jeevan Mission

Jal Jeevan Mission is envisioned to provide safe and adequate drinking water through individual household tap connections by 2024 to all households in rural India. The programme will also implement source sustainability measures as mandatory elements, such as recharge and reuse through grey water management, water conservation, rain water harvesting.

The Jal Jeevan Mission will be based on a community approach to water and will include extensive Information, Education and communication as a key component of the mission. JJM looks to create a jan andolan for water, thereby making it everyone's priority.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common queries about the Jal Jeevan Mission and water quality monitoring.

What is the Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM)?

Jal Jeevan Mission is a flagship programme of the Government of India aimed at providing functional household tap connections (FHTC) to every rural household by 2024, ensuring safe and adequate drinking water supply. The mission also focuses on source sustainability, water quality monitoring, and community participation.

How is water quality monitored under JJM?

Water quality is monitored at multiple levels: village-level testing using Field Test Kits (FTKs), block-level laboratory testing, and district/state-level NABL-accredited laboratories. Parameters tested include pH, turbidity, TDS, alkalinity, iron, fluoride, arsenic, nitrate, and bacteriological contamination as per BIS IS:10500 standards.

What is BIS IS:10500 and why is it important?

BIS IS:10500 is the Indian Standard for drinking water quality prescribed by the Bureau of Indian Standards. It defines acceptable and permissible limits for physical, chemical, and biological parameters. All drinking water supplied under JJM must comply with these standards to ensure public health safety.

How can I report a water quality issue in my village?

You can report water quality issues through the JalRakshak grievance form on this page, by calling the toll-free helpline 1916, or by contacting your local Village Water & Sanitation Committee (VWSC). Issues are routed to the concerned Public Health Engineering (PHE) department for remedial action.

What is a Village Water & Sanitation Committee (VWSC)?

A VWSC is a community-level body constituted under JJM to plan, implement, operate, and maintain village water supply schemes. It comprises elected representatives from the Gram Panchayat, women members, and community leaders, ensuring community ownership and sustainability of water infrastructure.

What are the common contaminants found in Indian drinking water?

Common contaminants include arsenic (found in West Bengal, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh), fluoride (Rajasthan, Telangana, Karnataka), iron (Assam, Odisha, Chhattisgarh), salinity (Gujarat, Rajasthan), and bacteriological contamination. JJM prioritises safe water supply in habitations affected by these contaminants.
